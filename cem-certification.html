Programme Overview

The Definitive Credential in Environmental Compliance

The Certified Environmental Manager (CEM) designation is the premier professional credential for practitioners who lead environmental compliance, risk management, and operational excellence within organisations. Awarded to individuals who demonstrate mastery across regulatory navigation, environmental risk assessment, auditing practice, and the operational integration of environmental management systems, the CEM signals a commitment to the highest standards of competence in a field central to licence-to-operate and long-term business resilience.

As environmental regulation intensifies — including Nigeria's NESREA Act and National Environmental Regulations, the ISO 14001 and ISO 19011 international standards, and IFC Performance Standards for project financing — organisations urgently need professionals who can interpret complex requirements, manage operational risk, and lead credible auditing and compliance programmes. The CEM equips practitioners to do precisely that.

Why the CEM Stands Apart

  • Rigorous, exam-based credential mapped to national and international environmental regulatory standards
  • Covers the full compliance lifecycle — from regulatory navigation to auditing and operational excellence
  • Grounded in real-world case studies and practitioner competencies
  • Internationally recognised and aligned with ISO 14001, ISO 19011, ISO 45001, and NESREA frameworks
  • Meets continuing professional development requirements for leading environmental and EHS bodies
Who Should Pursue It

Built For Environmental Managers

The CEM is designed for environmental managers, compliance officers, EHS practitioners, and operations leaders with a mandate to manage environmental risk and regulatory obligations.

A minimum of three years of professional experience in environmental management, EHS, or a closely related field is recommended before sitting the CEM examination.

  • Environmental Managers and Coordinators responsible for facility compliance, permitting, and programmes
  • EHS Managers and Directors seeking a formal benchmark of their environmental compliance expertise
  • Compliance Officers and Regulatory Affairs Specialists monitoring evolving environmental legislation
  • Environmental Health & Safety practitioners transitioning into broader environmental management roles
  • Operations and Facility Managers overseeing environmental permits, waste management, and emissions controls
  • Consultants and Auditors supporting organisations in environmental compliance and management systems
  • Policy Professionals and Regulators engaged in environmental enforcement or public-sector implementation
Eligibility

Eligibility & Prerequisites

To be eligible for the CEM examination, candidates must meet one of the following educational and experience pathways.

Pathway A
Bachelor's degree (or equivalent) in environmental science, engineering, or a relevant discipline plus a minimum of 3 years of full-time professional environmental management experience.
Pathway B
Master's degree or postgraduate qualification in environmental management, occupational health & safety, or a cognate field, plus 2 years of professional experience.
Pathway C
7 years of documented professional environmental management experience for candidates without a degree, substantiated by a supervisor attestation.
Experience Scope
Qualifying experience includes environmental compliance, risk management, auditing, permitting, or operational environmental management. Internships are credited at 50%.
Supporting Credentials
Holders of ISO 14001 Lead Auditor, NEBOSH Environmental Certificate, or an IEMA-accredited qualification may apply for accelerated review under the RPL scheme.
Curriculum

Core Competency Domains

The CEM curriculum is organised into five interconnected competency domains — a systems-level view of professional environmental management practice, from regulatory compliance to auditing and operational integration.

D1
Environmental Compliance & Regulatory Management
Navigating national and international environmental law and regulatory frameworks
  • Interpreting and applying environmental legislation, including NESREA regulations and sector-specific standards
  • Managing environmental permits, licences, and regulatory reporting obligations
  • Tracking regulatory change and assessing organisational compliance obligations
  • Developing and maintaining compliance calendars and documentation systems
  • Liaising with regulators, inspectors, and enforcement agencies
  • Managing non-compliance findings and corrective action processes
D2
Environmental Risk Management
Identifying, assessing, and mitigating environmental risk across operations
  • Conducting Environmental Impact Assessments (EIA) and environmental due diligence
  • Assessing site contamination, spill, and legacy liability risk
  • Applying risk assessment methodologies (HAZOP, bow-tie analysis, risk matrices) to environmental hazards
  • Developing environmental risk registers and mitigation action plans
  • Managing environmental liability and insurance considerations
  • Integrating environmental risk into enterprise risk management frameworks
D3
Environmental Auditing & Assurance
Planning, conducting, and reporting credible environmental audits
  • Applying ISO 19011 auditing principles to environmental management system audits
  • Conducting internal, supplier, and third-party compliance audits
  • Preparing audit plans, checklists, and evidence-based findings
  • Tracking corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) to closure
  • Supporting ISO 14001 certification and surveillance audit readiness
  • Communicating audit findings to leadership and regulatory stakeholders
D4
Operational Excellence & Management Systems
Embedding environmental management into day-to-day operations
  • Designing and implementing Environmental Management Systems (EMS) aligned with ISO 14001
  • Applying continuous improvement methodologies to environmental performance
  • Managing resource efficiency, waste minimisation, and pollution prevention programmes
  • Integrating environmental objectives into operational KPIs and management review
  • Leading cross-functional teams in environmental performance improvement initiatives
  • Building a culture of environmental accountability across operations
D5
Environmental Monitoring, Reporting & Emergency Preparedness
Ensuring accurate monitoring, reporting, and incident response capability
  • Designing environmental monitoring programmes for emissions, effluents, and waste
  • Managing environmental data quality, recordkeeping, and regulatory reporting
  • Developing spill response and emergency preparedness plans
  • Coordinating incident investigation, root-cause analysis, and reporting
  • Engaging communities and stakeholders on environmental performance and incidents
  • Preparing for regulatory inspections and third-party verification
Assessment

Examination Structure

The CEM examination is a rigorous, proctored assessment delivered in two parts, designed to evaluate both theoretical knowledge and applied professional judgement.

Part A — Knowledge & Application

A computer-based examination comprising 150 multiple-choice and scenario-based questions across 3 hours 30 minutes. Questions are distributed across the five competency domains as follows.

Domain 1: Compliance & Regulatory Management
Environmental legislation, permitting, regulatory reporting, NESREA compliance
24%
Domain 2: Risk Management
Environmental Impact Assessment, risk methodologies, liability management
20%
Domain 3: Auditing & Assurance
ISO 19011 auditing, ISO 14001 certification, corrective action management
20%
Domain 4: Operational Excellence
Environmental management systems, continuous improvement, resource efficiency
19%
Domain 5: Monitoring & Emergency Preparedness
Environmental monitoring, incident response, regulatory reporting
17%

Part B — Integrative Case Study

A structured written response examination (2 hours) analysing a complex, multi-stakeholder environmental compliance scenario — assessed on synthesis of domain knowledge, judgement, and a coherent corrective action plan. Case studies reflect real-world challenges such as:

  • Responding to a regulatory non-compliance notice with conflicting operational constraints
  • Designing a corrective action plan following a critical environmental audit finding
  • Leading an incident response and investigation following an environmental spill
  • Preparing an organisation for ISO 14001 certification within a constrained timeline

Examination Administration

  • Delivery: remote proctored (online) or in-person at accredited testing centres
  • Frequency: available quarterly (March, June, September, December)
  • Languages: English, French, Portuguese, Swahili (select markets)
  • Pass mark: 70% on Part A; 65% on Part B (both parts must be passed)
  • Retake policy: up to 3 attempts per 12-month period; 60-day cooling-off between attempts
Career & Organisational Benefits

A Mark of Excellence, Regionally Recognised

The CEM is recognised as a mark of excellence by leading employers, regulators, and industry bodies across the region. Research among hiring organisations indicates that CEM holders command a salary premium of 15–22% compared to peers without a professional environmental management credential, and are significantly more likely to hold senior EHS or compliance leadership roles.

For CEM Holders

  • Recognised credential validating regulatory, risk, and auditing expertise
  • Inclusion in the CEM Registry — a publicly searchable directory used by employers and regulators
  • Access to the CEM Professional Network: 8,000+ members across the region
  • Eligibility for the annual CEM Excellence Awards and featured case-study publication opportunities
  • Priority access to regulatory briefings, industry research, and emerging standards updates

For Employing Organisations

  • Assurance that environmental staff possess internationally benchmarked, current knowledge
  • Alignment with lender and investor environmental due diligence expectations (IFC, DFIs)
  • Supports regulatory compliance readiness, particularly under NESREA and ISO 14001
  • Demonstrates commitment to environmental talent development, strengthening employer brand
  • Corporate membership packages available for organisations sponsoring multiple CEM candidates

Standards

Code of Professional Ethics

All CEM holders are bound by the CEM Code of Professional Ethics, which establishes principles of integrity, competence, confidentiality, and public interest.

  • Act with integrity: provide accurate, evidence-based environmental compliance advice; do not misrepresent audit findings.
  • Maintain competence: keep knowledge current with evolving regulations, standards, and best practice through ongoing CPD.
  • Protect confidentiality: handle client and employer data responsibly and in accordance with applicable law.
  • Serve the public interest: prioritise actions that advance environmental protection and public safety.
  • Avoid conflicts of interest: disclose material conflicts and recuse from decision-making where independence is compromised.
Enrolment

Application & Registration

Applications are accepted on a rolling basis, with examination registration closing six weeks prior to each quarterly examination window.

1
Online Application
Complete the CEM candidate application via the certification portal. Upload educational credentials, CV, and employer attestation where required. Processing time: 10 business days.
2
Eligibility Review
The Credentialing Committee reviews submitted documentation and confirms eligibility or requests additional information.
3
Fee Payment
Upon eligibility approval, pay the examination fee. Students and candidates from LMICs are eligible for a 40% reduction.
4
Exam Scheduling
Once payment is confirmed, book your preferred examination date and delivery mode (remote or in-person).
5
Study & Preparation
Access to the official CEM learning platform, practice question bank, and study materials is granted immediately.
6
Examination
Sit Part A and Part B on your scheduled date. Part A results within 10 business days; Part B within 20.
7
Credential Issuance
Successful candidates receive a digital credential and are listed in the public CEM Registry within 5 business days.
